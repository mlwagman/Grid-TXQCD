\section{Setup}
The graded contour deformation multiplies the doubled auxiliary couplings by
$e^{\mp\eta\,p(t)}$, which in the chemical-potential frame is exactly a
per-timeslice weighting of the temporal hops of the doubled Wilson operator:
$w_f(t)=e^{-\eta\,\Delta p(t)/2}$ on forward hops (reciprocal backward and on
the conjugate block), i.e.\ an effective chemical potential
$\mu(t)=\eta\,p'(t)$ on the charge-conjugate block.  Measurement solves
invert the even--odd Schur complement $\Mt_{\rm pc}$ of the deformed doubled
operator; the correlator carries the analytic grading factor
$e^{-\eta[p(t_k)-p(t_s)]}$, verified to factorize from an
$\eta$-independent core at the $1$--$2\%$ level across
$\eta=0.2$--$0.6$ (envelope collapse; same configuration and source).

\section{The chemical-potential frame}
\label{sec:frame}
The deformation acts in the \emph{auxiliary frame} on the charge-2
cross blocks of the doubled operator.  Writing the doubled site
structure schematically in the (upper, lower) grading basis,
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm def}(\eta)\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
D_W + A & e^{-\eta\,p(t)}\,C \\[2pt]
e^{+\eta\,p(t)}\,\bar C & D_W^{*} + A^{T}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
with $D_W$ the Wilson--clover operator, $A$ the grading-diagonal
auxiliary couplings ($\sigma,\pi,s,p$), and $C$ ($\bar C$) the $d,n$
cross blocks.  The similarity transformation
\begin{equation}
G(\eta) = \mathrm{diag}\!\left(e^{-\eta p(t)/2}\,\mathbb{1},\;
e^{+\eta p(t)/2}\,\mathbb{1}\right),\qquad
\Mt(\eta) \;=\; G^{-1} M_{\rm def}\, G ,
\end{equation}
cancels the cross-block weights exactly (both $G$ factors act on the
same site, and $C$ connects upper$\to$lower):
\begin{equation}
\Mt(\eta)\;=\;
\begin{pmatrix}
G_u^{-1} D_W G_u + A & C \\[2pt]
\bar C & G_l^{-1} D_W^{*} G_l + A^{T}
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Because $G$ is site-diagonal it commutes with every site-local term of
$D_W$; the \emph{entire} deformation collapses onto the temporal hops.
For the forward hop of the upper block,
\begin{equation}
-\tfrac12(1-\gamma_t)\,U_t(x)\,\psi(x+\hat t)
\;\longrightarrow\;
e^{-\eta\,\Delta p(t)/2}\,\times\,
\left[-\tfrac12(1-\gamma_t)\,U_t(x)\,\psi(x+\hat t)\right],
\quad \Delta p(t)=p(t{+}1)-p(t),
\end{equation}
with the reciprocal weight $e^{+\eta \Delta p(t-1)/2}$ on the backward
hop and weights inverted on the conjugate (lower) block.  This is a
time-dependent \emph{real chemical potential}
$\mu(t)=\eta\,\Delta p(t)/2 \simeq \eta\,p'(t)/2$ coupling to the
doubling charge: links stay unitary ($w_f w_b = 1$ per link), the
site-diagonal blocks (mass, clover, all auxiliary couplings) are
untouched, and $\gamma_5$-hermiticity is broken in the standard
real-$\mu$ way, $\Mt^\dagger = \gamma_5\,\Mt(w\to 1/w)\,\gamma_5$
(equivalently the quasi-$\gamma_5$ identity
$\Gamma_5 \Mt(\eta)\Gamma_5 = \Mt(-\eta)^\dagger$, machine-exact in the
implementation).  All solver work happens in this frame: the
conditioning is set by $\mu(t)$, not by the exponentially large
aux-frame weights $e^{\pm\eta p}$.

\subsection{Criticality}
\label{sec:crit}
A uniform real chemical potential deconfines charge into the lowest
Matsubara-like mode once $\mu$ exceeds a critical value $\muc$ set by
the lightest charged excitation of the undeformed operator.  For the
graded profiles the local criterion
\begin{equation}
|\eta\,p'(t)| \;\le\; \muc \qquad \text{for all } t,
\qquad \muc \simeq 0.85 \ \ (\lambda=5,\ 16^3\times48)
\end{equation}
separates benign deformations from ones that manufacture near-zero
modes in every supercritical stretch.  For the cosine profile
$p(t) = \tfrac{T}{4}\left[1-\cos(2\pi t/T)\right]$ the maximal slope is
$\eta\pi/2$, so the threshold sits at
$\eta^{*} = 2\muc/\pi \approx 0.54$.  The near-zero mode count follows
a Fermi-surface--like threshold law,
\begin{equation}
N(\eta) \;\simeq\; c \sum_t \max\!\left[0,\;
\mu(t)^2 - M_*^2\right]^{3/2},
\qquad \mu(t) = \eta\,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,|\sin(2\pi t/T)| ,
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
with $c \approx 3.2$--$3.9$ fitted stably across counting cuts
(Sec.~\ref{sec:spectra}); the same law extrapolates to $\sim$85--105
modes at $\eta = 0.7$, exactly the shell that defeats every
$n_b \le 48$ coarse space (the ``window-volume'' wall of
Sec.~\ref{sec:mg}).  Fit versus measured counts at the
$\sigma < 10^{-4}$ cut (censored points excluded from the fit):

makes the light single-GPU multigrid of Sec.~\ref{sec:mg} possible.

\section{Cosine versus ramp contours}
\label{sec:cosramp}
At equal grading differential $\eta T/2$ the sharp ramp (tent) and
cosine profiles price very differently, and the difference is entirely
the localization physics of Sec.~\ref{sec:crit}: the tent's
gradient-sign flips bind wall modes at any $\eta$ (block-invisible to
multigrid transfer), while the cosine trades them for extended
supercritical-window modes that a coarse space \emph{can} carry ---
at the price of a steeper slope ($\pi/2$ vs.\ 1) and hence an earlier
criticality threshold:

\section{Mode localization: two mechanisms, two diagnostics}

\subsection{Gradient-sign flips bind wall modes (ramp profiles)}
Every sign flip of $p'(t)$ is a $\mu$-domain wall.  For the tent profile
$p=\min(t,T{-}t)$ there are two: the apex ($+\eta\to-\eta$) and the source
trough ($-\eta\to+\eta$).  Each binds a near-zero quasiparticle of one
grading block --- a \emph{degenerate pair} (apex: upper block; trough:
conjugate block).  Diagnostics that established the localization:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Adaptive-setup support collapse.}  Refining multigrid null
vectors through the solver concentrated their weight onto the wall slices;
in far blocks the remaining round-off support was renormalized to $O(1)$
noise by block orthogonalisation and the coarse correction injected it
volume-wide (solve residual diverged to $6\times10^6$ with a
well-conditioned coarse matrix).  Mixing refinements ($v\to v+z/\|z\|$)
restored stability --- the signature of modes narrower than the block.
\item \textbf{Source orthogonality with block structure.}  Wall-source
overlaps with the deep modes are $\lesssim10^{-6}$: the apex mode is
spatially separated from the source; the trough mode sits \emph{on} the
source slice but in the conjugate-block components.
\item \textbf{Rounding response.}  Quadratic rounding of half-width $w$ at
both walls lifts the pair: CG iterations at $\eta=0.8$ fall
$59{,}931\to 54{,}889/37{,}196/8{,}307/2{,}435$ for $w=2/4/8/12$; rounding
the apex alone leaves $\sigma_{\min}$ pinned at the trough state
($\times1.6$ lift only).  The sharp-tent ``onset edge'' at
$\eta\simeq0.85$ (a $\times66$ collapse of $\sigma_{\min}$ per
$\Delta\eta=0.05$) is \emph{wall physics}: the rounded profile passes
through it, reaching $\eta=0.9$ at $3{,}934$ iterations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Supercritical stretches bind extended modes (smooth profiles)}
With walls removed the remaining constraint is \emph{bulk} criticality:
wherever $|\mu(t)|=\eta|p'(t)|>\muc\simeq0.85$ over a stretch longer than
the quasiparticle formation length, near-zero modes form in that window.
Diagnostics:
\begin{itemize}
\item The cosine profile $p=(T/4)(1-\cos 2\pi t/T)$ (both walls maximally
smooth, full range $p_{\max}=T/2$, peak slope $\pi/2$) is clean while
subcritical ($756/1{,}149/2{,}113/5{,}204$ iterations at
$\eta=0.2/0.3/0.4/0.5$) but at $\eta=0.6$ ($\mu_{\max}=0.94$) costs
$26{,}761$ --- six times \emph{worse} than the sharp tent at equal
$\eta$ --- with no wall anywhere: the modes live in the supercritical
mid-regions.
\item For the rounded tent, $\mu$ is a triangle wave and the supercritical
exposure is $\Delta t = 2w(1-\muc/\eta)$ slices.  The iteration count
along $\eta=0.9\to1.2$ at $w=12$ ($3{,}934/7{,}074/16{,}683/49{,}639$)
ignites as $\Delta t$ crosses the formation length ($\sim$ few slices) ---
an accelerating exponential, not a cliff, because the exposure is tunable.
\item The multigrid window-volume law below.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Design rules} (measured): (i) keep
$\eta\,|p'(t)|\lesssim\muc$ everywhere; (ii) make gradient sign flips
smooth; (iii) subject to these, reach ($=\eta\times$ the profile
differential) is maximized by bang-bang slope saturation --- the rounded
tent family; the cosine spends its criticality budget on a slope peak.
Rounded-tent differential: $\eta\,(T/2-(w_{\rm apex}+w_{\rm src})/2)$;
the trough width is not reducible at deep $\eta$
($w_{\rm src}\!:12\to8$ costs $\times2.4$ at $\eta=0.9$).

\section{The multigrid scheme}
\label{sec:mg}
Two-level Petrov--Galerkin multigrid on $\Mt_{\rm pc}$ \emph{itself}
(never the normal operator: $\kappa$ un-squared, and eigenvector rather
than singular-vector transfer --- the normal-operator route fails at every
blocking).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Right basis} $V$: $n_b$ \emph{masked} inverse iterates.
A na\"ively converged inverse iterate is the \emph{wrong} test vector:
solving $\Mt v=r$ from white noise to even $10^{-3}$ collapses every
$v_i$ onto the supercritical band
($\|\Mt v\|/\|v\|=2.86$ vs.\ the bulk value $4.38$), the block
aggregation loses span, and the V-cycle \emph{amplifies} residuals
(probe $3.9$) --- measured on a controlled depth ladder
(converged\,$\to$\,$2.86/3.9$; 23 iterations\,$\to$\,$3.20/1.6$; 6
iterations\,$\to$\,$3.63/0.60$ but with a slow outer solve).  The
working basis applies $\sim130$ CGNR iterations and then a checkerboard
\emph{mask}: the vector is restored to the white-noise Rayleigh
quotient ($4.38$) --- spectrally re-whitened --- while retaining the
band's \emph{spatial envelope}.  Block over $4^3\times2$ (or $4^4$)
sites with all 48 internal components kept per site.  (Historically the
mask was discovered by accident --- a red--black-allocated field routed
through a full-grid checkerboard pick --- and then certified as the
recipe; the clean-recipe dial is retained for study.)
\item \textbf{Left basis for free}: the deformed operator obeys the
quasi-$\gamma_5$ identity
$\Gamma_5\,\Mt(\eta)\,\Gamma_5=\Mt(-\eta)^\dagger$ (machine-exact,
$4\times10^{-16}$, for every profile tested --- the dagger implementation
\emph{is} the weight-reciprocal toggle).  Hence left null vectors are
$u_i=\Gamma_5\,\Mt(-\eta)^{-1}r'_i$: no separate left-space machinery.
\item \textbf{Coarse operator} $A_c=U^\dagger \Mt V$ via Grid's native
two-subspace \texttt{CoarsenOperator} (phase-probe reconstruction;
81-point coarse geometry is required --- the Schur distance-2 footprint
crosses coarse corners; the stored coarse links are index-transposed,
calibrated against the operator to $10^{-15}$).
\item \textbf{Exact coarse solve, single GPU (final)}: with
$4^3\times4$ blocks, $A_c$ is $N=12{,}288$ dense ($2.4$\,GB), LU-factored
with \texttt{cusolverDnZgetrf} \emph{on the same GPU} as the fine stack
in $36$\,s; each V-cycle correction is a $\sim$ms \texttt{getrs}.  This
``MG-lite'' configuration is both lighter and \emph{better} than the
original two-GPU $4^3\times2$/$N{=}24{,}576$ layout it replaced
(Sec.~\ref{sec:iters}); the GPU-pairing constraint is gone.  Restarted
GMRES on $A_c$ converges at subcritical strengths (the historical
failure was the supercritical inherited-band system) but loses on
wallclock; it is retained as a zero-memory fallback.
\item \textbf{Shared null space}: at subcritical strengths the masked
basis is white-noise--Rayleigh and operator-generic, so it transfers
across profile offsets and deformation strengths intact: rebuilding
only the coarse operator and LU costs $36$\,s per node versus
$110$--$840$\,s of null-vector generation, with the one-V-cycle
contraction probe (and automatic full regeneration on failure) guarding
every reuse.  Measured: reused contractions $0.049$--$0.051$ vs.\ fresh
$0.048$--$0.078$ --- indistinguishable.
\item \textbf{Cycle}: minimal-residual smoothing (8 iterations,
non-Hermitian $z\mathrel{+}=\alpha r$, $\alpha=\langle Ar,r\rangle/\|Ar\|^2$)
before and after the coarse correction; flexible non-Hermitian PGCR
outer.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Performance} at the production-relevant point
(differential $14.4$): cosine $\eta=0.6$ + MG $=5{,}187$ fine operator
applications ($74$\,s/solve), vs.\ $53{,}522$ for CGNR on the same system
($10.3\times$) and vs.\ $8{,}958$ for the best plain-CG operating point
(sharp tent $\eta=0.6$): a $1.7\times$ net win with headroom in reach that
no sharp profile possesses.  Setup ($\sim6$\,min) amortizes over the
$\sim192$ solves per configuration.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Production certification.}  The scheme is packaged as a
pluggable odd-block solver
(\texttt{production/dtxqcd\_deform\_mg.h}\,$\to$\,\texttt{SetOddSolver})
and certified end-to-end in the measurement code at cosine $\eta=0.6$:
Gaussian (Hutchinson-class) solve $4{,}501$ fine operator applications;
wall-source propagator $8{,}695$ (vs.\ $26{,}761$ plain CG, $3.1\times$);
full baryon-correlator A/B against the plain-CG reference agrees to
$8\times10^{-7}$.  Every setup is gated in place: coarse-operator
consistency $A_c c = U^\dagger\Mt V c$ to $10^{-13}$, per-vector Rayleigh
prints, LU-diagonal conditioning, and a one-V-cycle contraction probe
($0.13$ healthy; $>1$ flags a collapsed basis before any solve is
attempted).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Window-volume law.}  At $n_b=16$ the coarse space holds
the supercritical band up to $\sim6$ slices of $|\mu|>\muc$: cosine
$\eta=0.6$ ($\sim6$ slices) converges; $\eta=0.7$ ($\sim13$) and $0.8$
($\sim17$) stall; the rounded tent at $\eta=1.2$ (a 7-slice triangle
peak) converges slowly.  The $n_b$ dial is now closed: $n_b=32/48$ and
deeper setup solves ($10^{-4}$/$10^{-5}$) do not dent the $\eta\geq0.7$
shell, and $n_b=128$ is infeasible in \texttt{GeneralCoarsenedMatrix}
(the $128^2$ per-site coarse tensor exceeds GPU kernel-launch limits) ---
$n_b=48$ is the practical span ceiling, so cosine $\eta\geq0.7$ stands as
a genuine wall of this two-level scheme.

\section{Reweighting-factor measurements}
\label{sec:rwf}
Correlated-sampling observables on the deformed contour require the
per-configuration reweighting factor
$\exp[\Delta\log\mathrm{Pf}(\eta)]$, computed as a line integral of the
stochastically estimated trace of the (even--odd reduced) logarithmic
derivative,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\log\det \tilde S(\eta)
\;=\; \int_0^{\eta} d\epsilon\;
\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\tilde S(\epsilon)^{-1}\,
\frac{d\tilde S}{d\epsilon}\right],
\qquad
\frac{d\tilde S}{d\epsilon}
= -\left(\partial_\epsilon\Mt_{oe}\,M_{ee}^{-1}\Mt_{eo}
+ \Mt_{oe}\,M_{ee}^{-1}\,\partial_\epsilon\Mt_{eo}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\tilde S = M_{oo} - \Mt_{oe} M_{ee}^{-1} \Mt_{eo}$ is the odd
Schur complement, $M_{ee}$ is $\epsilon$-independent (the deformation
rides only the time hops, so the even-block determinants cancel exactly
between deformed and undeformed contours), and
$\partial_\epsilon \Mt$ is the hop-form insertion with weights
$\mp\tfrac12 \Delta p(t)\,e^{\mp\epsilon\Delta p(t)/2}$.  The
$\epsilon$-integral is evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature over
nested \emph{segments} $[0,0.2]$, $[0.2,0.4]$, $[0.4,0.6]$ (two nodes
per segment, $x_q = a + (b-a)\{0.2113, 0.7887\}$, weights
$(b-a)/2$), so the target family $\eta \in \{0.2, 0.4, 0.6\}$ shares
segment estimates and the cost ledger is per-segment.  Sample counts
per node follow the Neyman allocation $N_q \propto w_q\,\sigma_q$ with
the measured node-variance table below.

\paragraph{Source-offset ratios by line integral.}
Source-centered contours require one factor per source offset $t_0$.
The offset is a \emph{continuous} parameter of the cosine profile, so
\begin{equation}
\log\frac{\mathrm{Pf}(\eta, t_0)}{\mathrm{Pf}(\eta, 0)}
\;=\; \int_0^{t_0} ds\;
\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\tilde S(s)^{-1}\,\frac{d\tilde S}{ds}\right],
\qquad
\frac{d\tilde S}{ds}\ \text{built from}\ \
\partial_s w_f = +\tfrac{\eta}{2}\,\Delta p'(t{-}s)\,w_f ,
\end{equation}
an insertion of size $\mathcal{O}(\eta\,p'')$ --- parametrically below
the $\eta$-insertion's $\mathcal{O}(\Delta p/2)$ at large $T$
(measured: per-sample $\sigma = 14$--$31$ vs.\ $\sim$540 at
$16^3\times48$; \emph{no} suppression at $4^4$, where
$p''\propto 1/T$ is large, as the scaling requires).  A uniform
$M=12$-node trapezoid rule (spectrally accurate for the smooth periodic
integrand) evaluated cumulatively yields \emph{all} 48 offsets at once;
periodicity supplies the exact sum rule $\oint ds\,(\cdot) = 0$, used
as a live gate and to Brownian-bridge--correct the accumulated error.
Offset coverage thus costs $\sim$2--3 anchor-equivalents instead of
$\times 48$.

\paragraph{Pricing.}  With the full stack, the $\eta=0.2$ target costs
$\approx 24$ GPU-hours per configuration for anchor precision
$\sigma \approx 1$ \emph{plus} all-48-offset coverage
($\sigma_D \approx 3$ at the antipodal offset); $\eta = 0.4$ costs
$\approx 2\times$; $\eta = 0.6$ is a $\sim$100-GPU-hour-per-configuration
subset campaign (its top node, $\epsilon = 0.558$, is marginally
supercritical).  A per-configuration anchor precision $\sigma \lesssim 1$
suffices because the exponentiation bias $e^{\sigma^2/2}$ of a noisy
weight cancels between numerator and denominator of reweighted ratios
for noise independent of the observable; the residual cost is effective
sample size, $\times e^{-\sigma^2}$.

\section{Bottom line}
Relative to the original Grid-sandwich deformed solves, the combined
kernel + even--odd + profile + multigrid program delivers
$\sim25$--$30\times$ at small $\eta$, converts $\eta\geq0.8$ from
unconvergeable to routine ($\eta=0.9$: $\sim2$ min/solve), and at matched
grading reach the smooth-profile multigrid beats every plain-CG operating
point.  The correlator inherits the profile grading analytically, so the
choice of profile is purely a (solver cost) $\times$ (SNR reach)
optimization --- with the cosine profile at $\eta\simeq0.6$--$0.75$
tangent to the ideal $\eta=0.9$ decay rate of the propagator part.  With
the $n_b$ dial closed, cosine $\eta=0.6$ + MG is the certified
production operating point; reaching the tangency region
$\eta\simeq0.7$--$0.75$ would need a qualitatively different coarse
space (e.g.\ deliberate multi-mask test vectors or $t$-adapted blocking),
not more of the same dial.

The reweighting-factor program (Sec.~\ref{sec:rwf}) closed the last
gap: what priced at $\sim\!10^5$ brute-force solves per configuration
and offset now runs at $\approx 24$ GPU-hours per configuration for the
$\eta = 0.2$ target with full source-offset coverage --- the product of
the Schur reduction, comb-4 dilution, loose sampling tolerance, the
single-GPU MG-lite solver with shared null spaces, Neyman-allocated
nested segment quadratures, and the continuous-offset line integral.
Every factor is an independently measured and gated number; the
combination converts the correlated-sampling program from a solver
demonstration into a running measurement.
